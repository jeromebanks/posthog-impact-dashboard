Domain Stewardship

Which parts of the codebase does this person's activity concentrate in strongly enough that they're one of the one or two most active people there right now.

The intuition

Some engineers are broad generalists; others become the person a team leans on for a specific area — auth, billing, the experimentation engine, whatever it is. Neither is better, but "who would you go ask about X" is a real, distinct kind of impact from either shipping code or reviewing it, and it's visible in the data as concentration of activity within a scope.

A naming correction. This component was originally called "Ownership / Trust Routing," which overclaimed what the underlying data shows. Authored-plus-reviewed activity in a scope is evidence that someone is currently active and concentrated there — it is not evidence that a team lead or process actively routed work to them, which would require data this dashboard doesn't have (assignment history, on-call rosters, team charters). "Domain Stewardship" describes what's actually measurable: activity concentration, not routing.

How a PR's scope is determined

PostHog's PR titles mostly follow a conventional-commit style — feat(scope): …, fix(scope): … — so the scope is parsed directly out of the title when present:

feat(billing): add proration for mid-cycle plan changes
      ^^^^^^^
      scope = "billing"

When a title has no scope prefix, the dominant top-level directory among the PR's changed files is used instead (e.g. most files under frontend/scenes/experiments/ → scope frontend). This fallback fires on a small minority of PRs — the current rate is shown in the dashboard's freshness line — and is disclosed rather than silently treated as clean signal.

The formula

for each scope (e.g. "billing", "flags", "ai-research"):
    rank engineers by (PRs authored in this scope) + (substantive reviews given in this scope)
    #1 in that ranking earns  +2 stewardship credit
    #2 in that ranking earns  +1 stewardship credit
    everyone else earns 0 for this scope

Engineer's raw stewardship = sum of credits across every scope they appear in

Domain Stewardship = percentile_rank(raw stewardship) across all engineers

Why top-2-per-scope, not a continuous share

A continuous measure (e.g. "what % of this scope's total activity is yours") would reward someone who does a little bit of everything roughly as much as someone who's clearly the go-to person for one specific area. Restricting credit to the top two per scope makes the signal binary and legible: either you're one of the one or two people most active in an area right now, or you aren't. It also means a single prolific generalist can't quietly accumulate credit across every scope just by touching all of them lightly — they have to actually be top-2 in each one.

Eli ranks highest here — not because of raw volume, but because he's a top performer in two different scopes rather than the single top performer in just one.

What's excluded going in

Mechanical PRs don't count toward any scope's activity, for the same reason they're excluded from Shipped Scope — a wave of dependency-bump PRs in a scope shouldn't manufacture stewardship credit for whoever happened to merge them.
