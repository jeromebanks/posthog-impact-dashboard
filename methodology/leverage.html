Leverage on Others

How much has this person helped teammates ship, measured by substantive code review — the clearest force-multiplier signal GitHub data can show.

The intuition

Shipping your own code is direct impact. Reviewing someone else's code well — catching a bug, pushing back on an approach, asking the question that saves a production incident — is leveraged impact: it improves work you didn't personally write, and it scales with how many different people you help, not just how much code you personally produce. This component tries to isolate that specific signal from the much noisier stream of "review" activity GitHub actually contains.

What counts as a review, here

Not every review submission counts. A review only qualifies if:

  • its state is APPROVED or CHANGES_REQUESTED — an actual verdict, not a bare comment thread; and
  • its body is longer than 20 characters — enough to rule out a bare "LGTM 👍" or "+1" as a substantive contribution.

Reviews from automated tools — CodeRabbit, Greptile, PostHog's own internal review bot (stamphog), and similar — are excluded entirely, on both sides of the calculation (as a reviewer, and as someone whose PR was reviewed).

The formula

substantive_reviews = count of qualifying reviews this engineer gave
distinct_authors    = count of different people whose PRs they reviewed

Leverage_raw = substantive_reviews × log(1 + distinct_authors)

Leverage on Others = percentile_rank(Leverage_raw) across all engineers

Why multiply by log(1 + distinct authors)

Ten substantive reviews spread across eight different teammates represents broader, more valuable help to the team than ten substantive reviews given to the same one person over and over — the second pattern looks more like a close mentor/mentee pair than org-wide leverage. The log1p term rewards breadth without letting it run away: going from reviewing 1 person to 8 people matters a lot; going from 8 to 16 matters less per person, which log-damping captures naturally.

Worked example

Two engineers, each with exactly 10 substantive reviews in the window:

EngineerReviewsDistinct authors reviewedlog(1+n)Leverage_raw
Concentrated — reviews mostly one teammate1010.696.9
Broad — reviews spread across the team1082.1921.9

Same review count, but "Broad" scores roughly 3x higher — the formula is measuring how many different people were helped, not just how many review actions were taken.

What this deliberately doesn't include: review speed. An earlier version of this metric added a small bonus for fast review turnaround, measured as time from PR creation to this reviewer's first review. That interval includes time before the reviewer was ever actually asked to look at the PR, so it wasn't measuring responsiveness — it was measuring something closer to "how quickly did anyone happen to look at this," which isn't attributable to a specific reviewer. Rather than ship a plausible-looking number built on a shaky timestamp, it was removed. Leverage on Others is currently pure count-and-breadth, nothing time-based.
